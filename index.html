2009

Chapter I

"What if we could simulate a molecule before we ever synthesize it?"

B.Sc. Chemistry → M.Sc. Computational Chemistry, University of Belgrade. The question that started everything: can math replace a test tube? Thesis: homology modeling of the D2 receptor. Spoiler — yes, sometimes.

  • Quantum mechanics on a laptop. Molecular mechanics. Force fields.
  • "In silico" is just a fancy phrase for data science with atoms.
  • The gap between a model and a result is almost always an engineering problem.
2015

Chapter II

"Eight years of hunting drug candidates with code."

Ph.D. in Pharmaceutical Chemistry — anti-Leishmanial drug discovery — at a government-funded national research institute [RS]. COST Action research visits: University of Siena and NIC Ljubljana. Six peer-reviewed publications. The scientific record is there; the engineering habit started here.

  • Cheminformatics pipelines: QSAR, virtual screening, docking, ADMET.
  • Structure-based drug design: enzyme modeling → hit identification → synthesis proposal.
  • Reproducibility is not a virtue — it's infrastructure.
  • First encounter with "scale": 100K ligand libraries don't fit in a spreadsheet.
  • Molecules 2016 — Arginase Flavonoid Anti-Leishmanial In Silico Inhibitors
  • Molecules 2018 — In Silico Discovery of anti-Leishmanial Quinalidine
  • Molecules 2019 — Oxadiazoles & Indolizines vs. Leishmania
  • Mol. Biochem. Parasitol. 2021 — Nicotinic AChR Virtual Screening
  • IJMS 2022 — LEA Proteins & Desiccation Tolerance in Ramonda serbica
  • IJMS 2023 — CRISPR/Cas9 Disruption of Arabidopsis thaliana DSS1
2018

Chapter III

"While writing a thesis, I built landing pages that outlived both of us."

Front-end developer — remote — for a US logistics marketplace [TX] and a product studio [RS]. SEO-optimized pages still live in production 7+ years later. Angel-backed → $5M ARR. The lesson: domain expertise without delivery is just potential.

  • First taste of shipping real things to real users — not academic artifacts.
  • "It works on my machine" is not a deployment strategy.
  • Performance, conversion, and accessibility are engineering problems, not aesthetic ones.
2019

Chapter IV

"Biology generates more data than any scientist can analyze manually."

Research Scientist on a government-funded project [RS]. Competitive funding — 10% acceptance rate. Transcriptomics, intrinsically disordered proteins, structural modeling. Stakes included Parkinson's disease research. Presented at academic conferences.

  • Genomics pipelines: from single-gene studies to whole-transcriptome analysis.
  • First real encounter with bioinformatics infrastructure — reproducible environments, workflow managers.
  • "When done right, biology is not the bottleneck. It's the pipeline."
2022

Chapter V

"10 million biomarker pairs. 2 days. Zero manual failures."

Bioinformatics Scientist at a bioassay R&D company [GB]. Slurm-based HPC pipeline with auto-partitioning for production-scale genomics. Led the SELEX aptamer program — biophysics, structural modeling, click chemistry. Refactored cross-language pipelines; cut RNA splicing runtime by 40%.

  • Scheduling 10M+ jobs on a cluster is not a research problem — it's an ops problem.
  • CI coverage in bioinformatics is not optional. It's how you trust your results.
  • Structure-guided aptamer design: biophysics, code, and chemistry at the same table.
  • Cross-language pipelines (R + Python + Groovy + Bash) — clarity of interface beats purity of language.
2024

Chapter VI

"4 ms median latency. 100% record coverage. Production or it didn't happen."

Full-Stack Data Engineer for a US clinical data company [NY] — remote. OMOP ETL sequence assembler: automated, audit-ready, zero manual failures. Serverless cross-storage workflow with SDK orchestration, real-time DB integration, and full traceability at 4 ms median latency.

  • OMOP CDM: the standard that makes clinical data comparable across institutions.
  • Regimen assembly: combinatorial logic + clinical constraints + auditability = genuinely hard.
  • "Large scale" means dependency-graph complexity, not just row count.
  • The build system is the product. If it can't be handed off, it isn't done.
